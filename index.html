How to use this trainer

Tap a world on the home page to open an area: Essentials, Neck or Ear training. The links at the bottom open How to use (this guide), Progress and Settings. The row of buttons under the title switches between the parts of an area, and the area remembers the part you used last. The gold pick at the top left brings you back home. Less-used choices are behind a small Options button. Every area has a ? button that opens its part of this guide.

Key Signatures

The chords of any key, with their Nashville numbers and diagrams. Under them is Common progressions: 1-4-5-1, 1-5-4-1, 1-4-1-1 and the other main ones, then the 12-bar blues (standard and quick change), all played in the key you pick.

How to start: Pick a key in the key bar, tap any chord card to hear it, or tap a progression to hear it played. The 12-bar chart (Bar 1 of 12 ...) appears only while a 12-bar blues is chosen. Tempo is next to the progressions; the cadence-first choice, light strum on beat 3 and the old Play key cadence button are behind Options.

Listen & Name

Ear training. Listen & Name plays a short progression and you tap the numbers you hear.

How to start: Tap Play first question at the top, then tap the numbers in order and press Check. Famous Tunes is the next button in the Ear training row. Mix two numbers up? Try Practice a pair.

Famous Tunes

Under Ear training. Simple songs everyone knows, shown as Nashville numbers so you can hear the 1, 4 and 5 in real music.

How to start: Pick a song, then choose Hear it, Name it or Which song. Next song is at the top. Tempo and melody/chords are under the song.

1-4-5 Map

Shows where all seven chords of a key sit on the neck in one small area, color-coded by number.

How to start: Pick a key, tap any shape or card to hear it, or press Play 1-4-5-1. The 7 can be shown as a m7♭5 (7ø) or a diminished triad (7°). Slide the box up or down a fret to change key. Try the quiz to find each number.

Triad cluster: the second button at the top shows the 1, 4 and 5 as small three-note shapes on the top three strings (G, B, E), with brackets for the fourth and the whole step. Pick C, D, E, F, G or A, tap a shape to hear it, or press Play 1, 4, 5, 1.

In position: the third button shows the 1, 4 and 5 as closed triads (using inversions) packed into one small window of the neck, so your hand barely moves; choose the low or high window, tap a chord to hear it with the others faded.

Theory

The Circle of Fifths: how keys are related and which chords belong together.

How to start: Tap a key on the circle to explore it, or press the tutorial button for a short step-by-step tour.

Chords, Scales, Barre chords & Triads

The shapes themselves. Essentials holds the basics: Key Signatures (the chords of a key and the common progressions), Chords (open and barre chord diagrams, then the five CAGED barre shapes with their triads) and Scales and modes on the full neck. Neck holds Triads, Full neck and the 1-4-5 Map.

How to start: Open Essentials and choose Chords or Scales, or open Neck and choose Triads, Full neck or 1-4-5 Map in the row under the title. Tap any card, dot or shape to hear it. The key bar sets the key. Full neck shows the 1, 4 or 5 chord all the way up the neck, as a portrait or a landscape neck, and its Cycle 1-4-5 button steps through the three chords for you.

Colors on the neck: red = the root (R), blue = the 3rd (♭3 in minor), amber = the 5th. The letter is printed on every dot as well.

Settings

Volume, the sound check page, and a button that forgets your remembered choices.

How to start: Open it with the gear at the top right or Settings on the home page.

Progress

Your scores from the games, saved on this device.

How to start: Open it any time from the Progress button at the top or the Progress link on the home page. It shows which numbers you mix up most.

Chord shapes

Numbers in the dots are fingers (1 = index). Major is shown three ways: the open (or usual) shape, the E-shape barre (root on the 6th string) and the A-shape barre (root on the 5th string). Every shape is checked note by note.

Barre chords & triads (CAGED)

Every chord can be played in five shapes up the neck, named after the open chords they come from: C, A, G, E and D. Inside each big shape is a small triad from Neck > Triads. Strum both to hear they're the same chord. Dots: red = root, blue = 3, amber = 5 (pale = the rest of the chord, solid = the triad).
